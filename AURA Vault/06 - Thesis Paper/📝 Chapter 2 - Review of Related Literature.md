## 2.1 Occupational Hazards and the Case for Automation
Systematic reviews and meta-analyses quantify painting-trade risk directly: Sekhar et al. (2024) report significantly elevated odds of cough, dyspnea, and wheezing plus reduced FEV1/FVC among VOC-exposed paint workers; Arrandale et al. (2025) find a pooled OR of 2.26 for new-onset adult asthma in painting occupations; Boadu et al. (2023) catalogue sixteen respiratory conditions across construction trades; Patel et al. (2024) identify 61 hazardous chemical species (including 2 Group 1 carcinogens) in coating systems; Bello et al. (2020) find 58.4% of painter urine samples exceed isocyanate biomonitoring guidance. Beyond chemical exposure, fall risk and overhead-motion musculoskeletal load round out the hazard profile (NIOSH, 2024). Philippine obligations are codified in DOLE DO-13 s.1998 and RA 11058.

## 2.2 Mechanical Wall-Painting Automation
Kumtole et al. (2022), Patil (2021), and Megalingam et al. (2020) form a first generation of XY/mecanum-based academic prototypes. Prakash et al. (2024) — corrected from an earlier "Sowmya et al." misattribution — lowers the entry barrier further with Arduino Uno + NodeMCU. Thale et al. (2022) and Shamseldin (2024) push into higher-stakes deployment (suspended high-rise painting; smart mobile X–Y platform at ~5 m²/hr). At the industrial end, Zhou et al. (2022) drives a 7-DOF manipulator via BIM + 3D reconstruction (46.3 m² in 59.3 min), and Al-Ayoub et al. (2024, *PaintBot*) automates start-point selection and stand-off maintenance. Even a locally accessible reference implementation — Vijaya Kumar et al. (2025), corrected from an earlier "RSIS International" organizational-author misattribution — reinforces the feasibility of entry-level control hardware. Review-level evidence (Cai et al., 2019; Attalla et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025 — 320% research growth 2015–2024 across 212 WoS publications; Brosque et al., 2022 — 25–90% less repetitive work, 72% less hazardous-task time across 10 robots/12 projects) confirms the field is active but fragmented. **Gap:** all of the above follow fixed, pre-programmed paths; none perceive the wall or reason about design.

## 2.3 Human–Robot Collaboration and Safety
M. Zhang et al. (2023) identify adaptive programming, HRI interfaces, and safety as the dominant research themes in construction HRC. Sun et al. (2023), Earnest et al. (2026, PRISMA-ScR synthesis of 104 articles), and Okpala et al. (2023, Delphi-derived 40-hazard taxonomy) reinforce that mechanical/psychosocial risk dominates when robots share human workspace. **Relevance to AURA:** framing AURA as a fixed-envelope apparatus (not a co-worker) minimizes this hazard class by design.

## 2.4 Spray Systems and Trajectory Planning
Rudzuan et al. (2019) and Kiran & Prabhu (2020) establish that gantry-mounted, multi-axis spray control improves finish quality but within static operating patterns. Tawade et al. (2024) validates XY-gantry kinematics for precise 2D control. Geometry-informed planning is where the real coating-quality gains show up: Chen et al. (2020, point-cloud slicing), Bastida et al. (2023, spherical-mesh trajectory GUI), Gabbar et al. (2024, ROS-integrated ARSIP), and Hua et al. (2024) — the last cutting coating-thickness variance from 51.9 μm² to 3.64 μm² via segmentation + sequence optimization. **Takeaway:** spray quality is a joint function of mechanism + planner + coating parameters, not the sprayer alone.

## 2.5 CNC/Arduino Hardware Precision
Because AURA runs an Arduino Mega + 3× TB6600 + NEMA 23 on a belt-driven gantry, this exact hardware class has direct benchmarks: Wahjudi et al. (2025) characterize the identical stack on a retrofitted CNC unit (≤24.5 μm/mm X-axis error, ≤20.7 μm/mm Y-axis, ~0.23 mm Z). Muas et al. (2026, CoreXY H-Bot) reports 99.04% accuracy at 0.219 mm repeatability. Suresh et al. (2025), Elgeme et al. (2025, PID/ESP32), Das et al. (2024, Arduino CNC writer), and Ademi et al. (2025, vibration/resonance characterization) round out realistic accuracy expectations for AURA's own motion benchmarking against ISO 9283:1998.

## 2.6 AI and Computer Vision for Wall Segmentation
Instance segmentation has matured through a well-documented lineage: **He et al. (2017) introduced Mask R-CNN**, extending Faster R-CNN with a parallel mask-prediction branch and topping all three COCO-challenge tracks — the foundational reference for pixel-level instance segmentation — with **UNet++ (Zhou et al., 2019)** and further architectural variants (Hassan et al., 2022) refining mask quality and inference speed since. Bjekic et al. (2023) is the direct precedent for AURA's task — CNN-based wall/non-wall pixel classification. YOLOv8 (Jocher et al., 2023) evaluated on COCO protocol (Lin et al., 2014) is now the reference real-time model: Zhang et al. (2024, YOLOv8-CM, 0.908 mAP tunnel defects), Lin et al. (2025, +4.2% mAP50-95 @ 156 FPS building defects), Wang et al. (2023, BL-YOLOv8 road defects), Guan et al. (2025, YOLOv8 + fine-tuned SAM). **Why YOLOv8 for AURA:** justifies the zero-shot-first strategy — COCO-pretrained backbones generalize well enough that fine-tuning becomes optional optimization, not a prerequisite (Ni et al., 2023; R. Zhang et al., 2023 survey).

## 2.7 Deep Learning + Spray-Control Integration
Tiboni et al. (2022, *PaintNet*) learns multi-path spray trajectories from 3D point clouds; Liu et al. (2024) couples semantic segmentation with trajectory optimization; Cheng et al. (2025) and He et al. (2026, <7% deviation, >99% coverage) extend this further — all on industrial hardware/3D sensing AURA cannot assume, but validating that spatial understanding → spray-control coupling is the right direction at 2D/undergraduate scale.

## 2.8 AI-Based Color Recommendation
Yuan et al. (2021, *InfoColorizer*) and Wu et al. (2023, AIGC-empowered color matching) show deep-learning palette recommendation is mature and user-validated. Koh (2023), Li et al. (2025), and Ananya et al. (2025) extend the space (palette→interior GANs; ML/DL color-harmony scoring; PCA+clustering+SVM aesthetic classification). **Gap AURA fills:** none of the painting-robot literature treats color recommendation as a first-class module — AURA does.

## 2.8b Color Preference by Demographic (Age & Gender) — ready to insert (2026-08)

>
> The condition on inserting this into the actual Chapter 2 document was "once the feature itself is implemented" — that happened the same day ([[🎨 Color Recommendation Module]] § As-built record), so **this section is now cleared to go into the manuscript**. Every study cited below maps to a row of the shipped `CATEGORY_BIAS` table, which is the honest version of this claim: the constants are hand-derived from these findings' *direction*, not fitted to their data.

Bogicevic et al. (2018) provide the most direct experimental evidence for gender-linked color preference in a room-design context: across 762 participants, male guests preferred "masculine" hotel-room color schemes while female guests rated masculine and feminine schemes equally. Age- and gender-specific preference is further quantified for children by Hao et al. (2025), whose 3–15-year-old sample preferred warm hues at moderate-low saturation (≈25/100) and high value (≈75/100), with boys preferring higher saturation than girls and preference for saturation decreasing with age — and extended into adolescence (12–16) by Jiang et al. (2020). Elderly preference is independently corroborated by three studies: Torres et al. (2020) tie warm-versus-cool preference to room activity (warm for activity rooms, cool for bedrooms, both genders), Li et al. (2022) find low-saturation, warm, bright tones preferred across 306 Chinese urban elderly respondents, and Rapuano et al. (2023) find the elderly weight color/material more heavily than younger groups when emotionally evaluating a space. Broader survey evidence (Voordt et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2009; Yıldırım et al., 2007) confirms age and gender are statistically significant factors in interior color preference across general populations, though the specific direction and magnitude vary with study population, room type, and culture.

**Gap AURA fills:** existing AI-based color-recommendation literature (§ 2.8) — and every painting-robot system reviewed in this chapter — generates or applies color without conditioning on occupant demographics. AURA's color recommendation module **takes a "for whom" input that biases the CIE LCh seed** (L\*, C\*, and hue angle) before harmony generation runs, making demographic personalization a first-class, cited design decision rather than an unexplained UI option. Alongside it, an optional reference image lets the occupant's own stated intent enter the same seed, blended 70/30 against the room's measured colour.

> [!warning] Wording caution when this goes into the manuscript
> Write the bias as **directionally** grounded in the evidence above, not as derived from it. The deltas are hand-set from the direction of published findings, not fitted to their data — and for `child_boy` / `child_girl` / `teen` the **saturation level was deliberately set above** what Hao et al. report (their HSV S ≈ 25/100 ≈ C\* 20; AURA uses C\* 44–56), as a design judgement that a wall at the cited level reads flat for a children's room.
>
> A sentence like *"biases the harmony parameters per the evidence above"* would over-claim for those three rows. Safe phrasing: the **direction** of each bias (warmer, lighter, more or less saturated, by age and gender) follows the cited findings, while the **magnitudes** are design parameters this study sets and the ISO/IEC 25010 evaluator rating is intended to inform. Belongs in Chapter 5 limitations too. Full record: [[🎨 Color Recommendation Module]] § Second fix round.
